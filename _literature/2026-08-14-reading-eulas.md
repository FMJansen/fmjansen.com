## Summary
The [Living contracts](../living-contracts) paper referenced one which finds that only .13% (a bit over 1 in 1000) of people who look for and buy software actually read the terms (EULA). 

Apparently, there is an idea that if only enough buyers read the terms, companies with bad terms will get punished into improving their terms. This would require a way larger group; they cite a study by Schwartz and Wilde (1979) which mentions 20-30 percent of people who not only read, but are meaningfully informed about the terms.

## Other findings
They also see that:
- Bigger companies (in revenue, so probably more known) might ‘benefit’ from brand recognition in lower EULA reading rates (which could mean more trust)
- Free software might be more suspicous; at least the terms are read slightly more often
