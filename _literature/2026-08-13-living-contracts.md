## Summary
Huang et al. interviewed people about their experiences with (lease) contracts and probed them with three ways to get more out of a contract. I like taking the idea that a contract should be an artefact of an understanding between people and using that to get more use out of a contract.

## Findings
Some conclusions are quite straightforward, but great to have some evidence of, anyway. For example, people try to read or skim important contracts, but the legal jargon, length and complexity prevent them from understanding the contract properly (4.1.2). The power dynamics, situation of the renter (4.1.4), lack of knowledge and legal vagueness (4.1.3) also make it harder to act on ‘bad’ contracts (negotiate about the terms, take a different offer).

## Concepts
The information cards in the LeaseRead probe with e.g. context about the local law seem like an elegant intervention. I have more doubts about the other two probes (partly because of the risks (4.2.4), but also because it requires the term specifying such as a landlord party to cooperate).
